Profile

I’m Chuangqi Lee, a researcher with a background in computer science. My current interests lie at the intersection of human–computer interaction, AI for Medical, and Biomedical Engineering. I want to explore how intelligent systems can be designed around people and contribute to health and well-being.

My previous research has involved multimodal learning engagement assessment, body-based interaction, language-model agents, and visual perception. These projects have shaped my interest in combining algorithm development with interaction design and evaluation in real-world settings.

Alongside my research, I am the founder of Beijing Qichuang Era Technology Co., Ltd. and lead the Neuroboost project. I work on product definition, overall project development, and resource coordination, exploring how medical AI can be brought into practical software and hardware products.

Project experience

Perception in challenging driving scenarios

Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences

Collected driving cases and evaluated perception systems with MMDetection, MMDetection3D and SparseBEV to identify failure modes.

Advisor: Dr. Yonglin Tian. The project explored a specialized dataset to address gaps in existing perception models.

Personalized mental health agents

Tsinghua University

Explored personalized responses using interview and survey data, retrieval-augmented generation and a RedisVL vector database.

Advisor: Prof. Jiangtao Gong. Research focused on a large language model system for responding to psychological concerns.

Interpretable perception with Eloss

Tsinghua University

Investigated an information-theoretic loss metric and a complementary training strategy for abnormal data in complex traffic scenes.

Advisors: Prof. Xinyu Zhang and Dr. Shiyan Zhang. The source CV reports a 3% improvement in recognition accuracy and an Eloss response exceeding 100 times the standard value on anomalous data.

Personalized driving decisions with LLMs

University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee

Combined text representations of driving data with federated learning to investigate personalized driving styles.

Advisor: Prof. Shuaiqi (Roger) Shen. The project converted nuScenes data into text and combined personalized data with a central open-source model.

Understanding classroom engagement

Henan Normal University

Combined facial expressions, body movement and head pose to assess classroom participation through multimodal features.

Advisors: Prof. Dong Liu and Prof. Hui Liu. The system used XGBoost, ResNet and Perspective-n-Point. The source CV reports 20% higher average final-exam scores among students using the system.
